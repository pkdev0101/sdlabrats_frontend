    <h2 id="approach-title">Three kinds of engagement</h2>
    <p>Our teaching develops self, social, and custodial engagement. It helps kids discover their passions, build strong friendships and sportsmanship, and meet role models from the scientific community.</p>
    <p>Our learner-centered courses and camps deepen each student's interest in science and technology, with a curriculum as flexible and varied as our students' interests and talents.</p>

  <div>
    <h2 id="self-title">Self engagement</h2>
    <p>Education research suggests the most effective way to improve academic performance is to meet the intellectual and emotional needs of students as individuals, rather than chase higher average test scores across large groups.</p>
    <p>Overcoming life's obstacles takes grit. We work to build in each student a lasting commitment to becoming literate in STEM, using proven practices from cognitive psychology to help kids truly master STEM content and develop scientific habits of mind.</p>
  </div>

<section class="labrats__section labrats__split" aria-labelledby="social-title">
  <div>
    <h2 id="social-title">Social engagement</h2>
    <p>People tend to succeed when family and close friends support them. So we build friendship and sportsmanship into the program, and students support each other while working toward a shared goal.</p>
    <h2 id="custodial-title">Custodial engagement</h2>
    <p>We give students the best role models the scientific community has: scientists teaching science from the ground up, not starting in college. Our mentors know from experience what it takes to earn a science degree and what it means to be a scientist. They teach the commitment it takes to succeed in science, and the ethical standards the scientific community depends on.</p>
